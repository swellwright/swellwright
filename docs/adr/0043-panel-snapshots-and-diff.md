# ADR 0043: Snapshots of the panel and the diff between two of them

Status: accepted

## Context
The panel is the source of truth for its objects and Swellwright keeps snapshots, intents and a journal (`docs/en/design/00-index.md`, principle 12). ADR 0041 gave Swellwright a connection to a Remnawave panel and normalised, masked views of its objects with a hash of each object's masked configuration "for the snapshots to come". This slice builds them: a snapshot of everything the token may read, kept on this server, and a diff that says what changed between two snapshots or between a snapshot and the panel as it is now. The design is `04-remnawave-integration.md` (section 4, the "panel as source of truth" model), `02-data-model.md` (`PanelSnapshot`), `03-scenarios.md` (P18) and `05-security.md`. The later users of the same pieces are the convention linter, the impact calculator (a before/after of a profile edit is a diff), the "snapshot before a change" of the workflows (phase 4) and "snapshots restore a single object" (phase 7).

The documents disagree on what a snapshot may hold. `02-data-model.md` says "encrypted" in the entity table and, in the retention section, that secret values are "never written to logs, events, or snapshots (checked on write)"; P18 says "with secret masking or encryption"; and ADR 0041 said that a snapshot "stores what it needs to restore from, which is the unmasked object, encrypted". This ADR settles it.

## Decision

### A snapshot holds masked values only
A snapshot is the **masked, normalised configuration** of every object, in the form of ADR 0041 (every secret replaced by `[masked:<keyed fingerprint>]`). No unmasked value is read into, kept in or exported from a snapshot. This supersedes the sentence of ADR 0041 above, for these reasons:

- What a snapshot is for in this slice (a list of what exists, the diff, "what changed since", the drift view of later phases) needs only the masked form; a rotated secret shows as a change through its fingerprint, which is the point of the keyed fingerprint.
- Restoring a secret-bearing object (a profile with its REALITY key) is phase 7. It needs a write client, an approval and a privileged raw read of the object, none of which exists, and the adapters of ADR 0041 deliberately drop the fields they do not know, so a view could not restore an object whole in any case. When restore is built it needs a **live secret source**: the secret values of an object that was not rotated are read from the panel at restore time (the fingerprint says whether they changed), and a rotated one comes from the vault. If that turns out to be too weak, an optional encrypted blob under the vault's data key, shown only to `vault:reveal` with a step-up, can be added then as one more nullable column; nothing here prevents it and nothing needs it now.
- A store that holds every REALITY private key, short id and client credential of a fleet in one table is the most valuable thing in the database after the vault itself. A snapshot taken by the background job would hold it all the time, and an export would carry it out. Holding masked values only makes a snapshot, an export and a backup of the database safe to hand around, which is what GitOps (P18) wants.

So `02-data-model.md` stays right in its retention line, and "encrypted" in the entity row is not implemented: the content is compressed, not encrypted, because it is not secret.

### What it holds
Per kind of object, a list of `{ id, name, hash, config }`: `nodes`, `hosts`, `configProfiles` (with the Xray configuration and the raw inbounds, read by one detail request per profile because the list does not carry them), `internalSquads`, `externalSquads`, `subscriptionTemplates`, `subscriptionSettings`, `snippets` (identified by name), `nodePlugins`, `infraProviders` and `billingNodes`. API tokens, users and the system counters are not part of it. `config` is exactly the content that the view's `hash` covers (ADR 0041): the projection is the same object, and a test checks `hash === contentHash(config)` for every kind against the views the read routes return. So runtime state (online flags, versions of a node and of Xray, counters, traffic used, host facts, member counts, timestamps) is not in a snapshot and cannot make one differ from another.

A kind the token cannot read is `denied` (403), one the panel answers in a shape this release does not know is `failed` (with the code), and a panel that is down, refuses the token, asks to slow down or runs a version outside the window fails the whole read with the panel's code. A kind that is not `ok` is left out of the snapshot and of every comparison, said in `coverage`, in `complete: false` and in a note of the diff. A snapshot of nothing (every kind denied) is refused with 502 `panel_forbidden`.

### Hashes
Each object has the hash of ADR 0041. A kind has the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of its sorted `[id, hash]` pairs, and the snapshot has one over all covered kinds (`overallHash`). Two snapshots with the same overall hash hold the same configuration; the background job uses it to skip a snapshot when nothing changed.

### The key of the masks, and a key that changes
The fingerprint in a mask is keyed by `SW_AUTH_KEY` (ADR 0041), so a rotation of that key changes every fingerprint and every hash once. Each snapshot therefore keeps `maskKeyId`, a public 12-hex-digit HMAC of a constant under that key (it reveals nothing about the key). When two sides of a diff have different ids, the diff compares `[masked:xxxxxxxx]` as `[masked]`, says `mask_key_differs`, and finds every other change as usual: a rotated secret cannot then be told from an unchanged one, and the note says so, instead of showing every secret of the panel as rotated. In development, where the key is made at start, every restart is such a case.

### Storage
Two tables (`packages/db`, ADR 0013): `panel_snapshots` (the summary: id, panel URL, label, kind `manual | scheduled | before_change | imported`, time, who (id and the login name at the time, no foreign key, like the audit chain), panel version, counts and coverage per kind, object count, hashes, `maskKeyId`, sizes, and `checkedAt` with `changesSince`) and `panel_snapshot_contents` (one row per snapshot: the JSON of `{ format, schemaVersion, maskKeyId, kinds }` gzipped, `bytea`, with the SHA-256 of the stored bytes, cascade on delete). The list reads only the first table. The content is checked against its hash when it is read; a row that does not match, does not decompress (the output is bounded) or is not a snapshot is 409 `snapshot_corrupt`, and the summary still reads. A snapshot belongs to a panel by its URL and not to a connection: a connection row is replaced when the token is rotated, and the history must survive that, so `connectionId` is a note without a foreign key. The list shows the snapshots of the connected panel (or all of them while nothing is connected: the history is kept); comparing with a snapshot of another panel is 409 `snapshot_other_panel`.

A snapshot is refused with 413 `snapshot_too_large` when its JSON is larger than `SW_PANEL_SNAPSHOT_MAX_MIB` (32 by default; a synthetic panel of 800 nodes and 800 hosts is 1.4 MiB of JSON and 100 KiB stored, read in about 0.3 s and compared with itself in 10 ms). `before_change` and `imported` are reserved: nothing creates them yet. "This snapshot is the rollback anchor of change X" is not modelled; the later change flow will name its snapshot.

### Retention
After every snapshot the newest `SW_PANEL_SNAPSHOT_KEEP` (50) of that panel stay, and so does the newest of each UTC day of the last 14 days, and a `before_change` snapshot is never removed by retention (the change flow decides when an anchor is released). Everything else is deleted, each deletion in its own transaction with a `panel.snapshot.deleted` record (`reason: retention`, no actor). The 14 days are a constant, not a setting.

### The background job
`SW_PANEL_SNAPSHOT_INTERVAL_HOURS` (24; 0 switches it off). `PanelSnapshotScheduler` ticks every quarter of an hour at most and asks the service whether a snapshot is due: the time since the later of the newest snapshot and the last time the panel was found equal to it, against the interval, so a restart does not push the next snapshot a day away. A due round reads the panel (the vault must be open: the token comes from it; a sealed vault is a silent skip), and when the overall hash equals the newest snapshot's it only records `checkedAt` and `changesSince = 0` on that row ("checked, no change": nothing is written to the audit chain), otherwise it stores a `scheduled` snapshot and prunes. A round that fails is logged and the next one tries again; a round that is running is not started twice; shutdown waits for it. The clock is injectable (`AppOptions.snapshotClock`), and the tests move it.

### The diff engine
A pure function in `packages/remnawave` (`diffSnapshotContents`, `diffValues`), tested without a database. Objects are matched by id, the same hash means unchanged, and otherwise the two `config`s are walked together. Rules that keep the result readable:

- an array of objects is matched by its natural key, the first of `tag`, `uuid`, `ruleTag`, `name` that every element has once (`inbounds[tag=MAIN]`), so adding an inbound is one change and not a shift of every index; the order of the common elements is reported as a change for an array where order may matter (outbounds, rules), not for a set of objects (`inbounds`);
- a known set (`tags`, `nodeUuids`, `serverNames`, `alpn`, ...) is compared without regard to order and reports the members that came and went; a node whose tags were only reordered has another hash and no change, which is the `noise_only` note;
- any other array is compared by its longest common subsequence (positions beyond two million table cells fall back to positions), so one inserted rule is one change, and what is removed and added at the same place is paired into one change of that position;
- a list of `clients`, `users`, `accounts`, `peers` or `proxies` is never shown, only counted (`{ items: n }`, `hidden`), including inside a value that is shown whole;
- a masked value compares by its fingerprint: a rotated secret is `changed` with `masked: true` and the two fingerprints, and no value;
- a text of several lines (a YAML template) shows the lines that went and the lines that came;
- timestamps at the top of an object are ignored (the projection leaves them out already);
- the result is capped: 1000 objects, 200 field changes per object and 4096 characters per value; the counts are always complete, `truncated`, `valueTruncated` and the `objects_cut` note say what was cut.

"No differences" is a result (`identical: true`, every count, an empty list), not an empty answer. Paths are `config.inbounds[tag=MAIN].port`; `labels` names the uuids that appear in the values (a node, a squad, `Profile / TAG` for an inbound) so that a change can be read without a second lookup. Property tests (a seeded generator, no new dependency) check that a value compared with a copy of itself differs nowhere, that a mutation is found exactly when it changes the value, that swapping the sides swaps additions and removals, that a set is the same in any order, and that what one side adds the other removes. Tests against the fake panel check each edit a person can make (an address changed, an inbound added, a node removed, a template body changed, a secret rotated, a client added, the runtime changing by itself, a kind denied) and an 800-node panel.

### Routes, permissions, codes
`GET /panel/snapshots` (`panel:read`, `auth: any`, paged with `limit` and `offset`, newest first, with the settings of the job and the retention), `POST /panel/snapshots` (`panel:write`, session only, `{ label? }`, 201; a forced read, counted against the refresh limit of the person), `GET /panel/snapshots/{id}` (`panel:read`), `DELETE /panel/snapshots/{id}` (`panel:write`, session only, step-up, 204), `GET /panel/snapshots/{id}/diff?against=live|<id>&refresh=&includeUnchanged=` (`panel:read`; `live` reads through the memory of this server like every read, `refresh=true` asks the panel again under the same limit; a comparison with the live panel is stored on the snapshot as `checkedAt` and `changesSince`) and `GET /panel/snapshots/{id}/export` (`panel:read`, a JSON file, `Content-Disposition: attachment`). A Swellwright API token with `panel:read` reads, lists, compares and exports; it cannot take or delete (those routes are session only, and a step-up cannot be given to a token). Failures of the panel use the codes of ADR 0041; new codes are 409 `snapshot_other_panel`, 413 `snapshot_too_large` and 409 `snapshot_corrupt`. OpenAPI 0.25.0.

A comparison with the live panel while the vault is sealed is answered from the memory of the server when it holds a fresh read, and with 423 `vault_sealed` when a read is needed.

### Audit
`panel.snapshot.created` (strict, in the same transaction as the row: actor or none, `kind`, the label, the panel version, the object count and the count per kind, `overall_hash`, `complete`, the sizes) and `panel.snapshot.deleted` (`reason` `user` or `retention`). No object, no configuration and no masked value is in a record; a test scans every record, response and export for every secret of the fake panel and of the rotations. Reads, comparisons and exports are not recorded (the same as reads of the panel in ADR 0041: what was read can be read again, and an export holds nothing a read route does not return).

### The console
Panel > Snapshots (`/dashboard/panel/snapshots`, `panel:read`): the header card with compare, refresh and "take a snapshot" (teal, last, `panel:write`; disabled with the reason while no panel is connected), then a titled card with the panel's table (`@kastov/mantine-react-table-open`, paged on the server): taken, name and who, source chip, panel version, objects (with a warning mark for an incomplete one), changes since (a chip: none, a number, or a dash), size, and row actions: details, compare with the panel now, download, delete (a confirmation, then a code). The details drawer shows the facts, the counts per kind with what could not be read, and the hashes. The comparison is a wide right-hand drawer: two selects (the snapshot, and "the panel now" or another snapshot), "ask the panel again", a strip of four metric cards (added, removed, changed, unchanged), the notes as callouts, and a table of the objects that differ whose rows open into the field changes (path, change, before, after; a secret shows its fingerprint and a "secret" chip, never a value). Empty, not-connected and failed states use the panel's components, texts are English and Russian, and the Overview gets a "last snapshot" card (time ago and changes since, read from this server's records, never from the panel).

## Consequences
- A panel can be snapshotted by hand or on a schedule, and two snapshots, or one and the live panel, compared object by object, without a secret appearing anywhere. Drift, the convention linter and the impact calculator can use `diffSnapshotContents` and `readPanelState` as they are.
- A snapshot cannot restore a secret-bearing object by itself (phase 7 and a live secret source).
- A change of `SW_AUTH_KEY` makes the next comparison across it blind to rotations, and says so.
- The fake panel gets mutation helpers (`packages/remnawave/src/test-support/snapshot-mutations.ts`), the reader gets `state()`, and `PanelClients` the `maskKeyId`.

## Not built here
Restore of an object (phase 7), YAML export and Git (P18), a snapshot before a change and its link to a change (phase 4), weekly snapshots beyond the retention above (the design table says "daily for 90 days, weekly for 2 years": the count and the 14 daily days replace it until there is a reason for more), partial snapshots of one object, an encrypted blob, several panels in the list (the model allows them), and a diff of the computed configuration.
